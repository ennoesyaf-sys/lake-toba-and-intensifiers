GRAMMAR · P. 37

What are intensifiers?

An intensifier is a word used to make another word (usually the one right after it) sound stronger or weaker. It has little meaning on its own and can often be left out, but it helps express ideas more clearly. Intensifiers are usually adverbs or adverbial phrases.
very, really, absolutely, extremely, highly, quite, rather, so, too, totally

GRAMMAR · P. 38

Three key points when using intensifiers

Use them only when necessary. If you use “very” only once, readers understand it truly means “very”.
✗ The new boss is very nice, very friendly, and incredibly smart. (overusing intensifiers reduces the effect)
Doubling suggests you are not choosing words carefully. It may be acceptable in casual writing but is incorrect in formal contexts.
✗ I’m very very good at acting.
Non-gradable adjectives (impossible, unanimous, dead) already express an extreme state, so they cannot be made stronger or weaker. Something can’t be “very dead”. Use non-grading adverbs such as quite, absolutely, nearly or almost.
✗ The water is rather boiling.
✓ The action is quite impossible.
